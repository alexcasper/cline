# Introduction

Small research groups rarely fit the hosted-conference product market. Their needs are idiosyncratic, their budgets thin, and their tolerance for vendor lock-in low. The alternative—building from scratch for each event—repeats the same work every year.

Our answer is a *template, not a product*: one Django codebase in which every venue-specific fact lives in a single configuration file.

# Design

The system separates three concerns:

1.  **Configuration.** Name, dates, tracks, rooms, deadlines.

2.  **Content.** Submissions, reviews, decisions.

3.  **Presentation.** Templates and a house stylesheet.

# A Note on Rendering

Papers are stored as LaTeX and rendered to PDF, HTML, and markdown from the same source. The render quality of inline mathematics such as
``` math
T_{\text{setup}} = \alpha \cdot c_{\text{venues}} + \beta
```
matters as much in a browser as on paper, which is why the house style ships three implementations of one visual language.

# Conclusion

Configurable single-template systems are viable for small fleets. The discipline that matters is not technical: it is the willingness to say no to hardcoded venue details.
