SwarmShape: Influence Structure in LLM Agent Swarms of Size 2–30 Replicates Across Serving Stacks; Conduct Rates Do Not

When an LLM agent swarm misbehaves, blame usually lands on a bad actor. We ask — Nov 2026 · CC BY 4.0

Abstract

When an LLM agent swarm misbehaves, blame usually lands on a bad actor. We ask a different question: how much of a swarm's behaviour is a property of its *shape* — who can talk to whom — rather than of any individual agent? We adapt an LLM market harness (agora) so that seller swarms of size 2–30 communicate over 57 distinct undirected communication graphs (exhaustively enumerated up to size 8, family-sampled above), give every agent the same unenforced promise — never charge buyers more than unit cost plus a markup — and detect promise- breaking from committed prices, never from transcripts. We run the full 114-game grid twice, on two independent serving stacks (llama.cpp with a 4-bit quant on dusk; vLLM on full-precision weights on Modal), plus 4-seed and 10-seed campaigns. One result reproduces across stacks. Who-follows-whom is concentrated in every game (101/101 dusk games exceed the uniform baseline), but most of that concentration is predicted by the graph's visibility structure alone: a neighbor-uniform null explains the bulk of it (median observed/null ratio 1.12 dusk, 1.07 Modal), leaving a modest behavioral excess — agents follow some neighbours more than visibility predicts — in 73%/66% of games, stable across stacks. Conduct rates do not reproduce: the clean-swarm promise-breaking rate is 2.5% of agent-rounds on dusk and 0.1% on Modal; the instructed rogue's own rate is within 2× across hosts (0.064 vs 0.039–0.051), so the popular "rogue lift" ratio is ill-defined; and dusk's action-level collapse at n $\geq$ 28 vanishes on Modal. The oversight lesson: structural influence metrics travel across serving stacks; conduct baselines must be established per stack, because an LLM swarm's apparent lawlessness can be an artifact of how the model is served.
